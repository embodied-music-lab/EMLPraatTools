# Memo — settlement items 2 and 5 both presuppose a definition of "the public surface" that does not exist

Opus, 1 September 2026. I need a ruling before the rename wave, and the
question turned out to be different from the one I set out to ask.

## What I was going to ask

Who picks the canonical words. You ruled the pattern — canonical word-based
names across the public surface, no wrappers, Table S2 documents them — and
nobody has ruled the actual names. They freeze with the paper, so I did not want
to invent them and present them as settled.

That question stands, but it is the second one.

## What I found looking for the list to rename

**There is no definition of the public surface anywhere in the plugin.**

- `plugin_EML_StatsGraphs/scripts/eml-lib-user.praat` is GENERATED by
  `setup.praat` at launch, gitignored, and contains nothing but ten `include`
  lines naming files. It exposes no procedure list.
- Nothing else enumerates a public API. There is no manifest of intended entry
  points, no naming convention that separates public from internal, and no
  access marker.
- Measured: **357 procedures match `eml[A-Z]...`** across the stats files. All
  of them are reachable by any script that includes the barrel, because Praat's
  `include` is a parse-time text paste and everything in scope is callable.
- The **15 `emlRun*Analysis` procedures** are the evident user-facing entry
  points by convention and by what the kit drives. But that is a convention a
  reader infers, not a boundary the code draws. `@emlCholeskySolveMulti` and
  `@emlRunAnovaAnalysis` are equally public in every sense the language enforces.

## Why this blocks two settlement items rather than one

**Item 2** says rename the public surface to canonical word-based names.
Renaming a set requires knowing its membership. Renaming all 357 is a different
project from renaming 15.

**Item 5** says the registry is the single source of the public surface, and
docs, the recorder and the barrel generate from it. A registry cannot be the
single source of a set nobody has defined. Item 5 is not implementable until the
set exists — and once it does, item 5 becomes the thing that ENFORCES it, which
is a considerably more valuable outcome than a generated barrel.

And **Table S2** documents the canonical names. Its row count is a direct
function of this ruling. Fifteen rows and 357 rows are different papers.

## What I think the ruling has to contain, in order

1. **The membership rule.** What makes a procedure public. The candidates I can
   see: the 15 `emlRun*Analysis` entry points alone; those plus the graphing
   entry points; or an explicitly curated list that may include kernels a
   sophisticated user would legitimately call directly.
2. **The mechanism.** Since the language enforces nothing, "public" has to mean
   something the registry can check — a marker in the source, a curated list in
   the registry, or naming convention promoted to a rule with a validator behind
   it. Otherwise the boundary erodes the first time someone adds a procedure.
3. **Then the names**, for whatever set 1 defines.

## On the naming itself

Once the set is known, I can produce a proposed name for each with its rationale
in one pass, cheaply, for you and Ian to accept or amend line by line. What I
should not do is choose them silently — they are on Ian's product and they
freeze with the paper.

One observation, offered rather than urged: if the public set turns out to be
the 15 entry points, the existing names are already close to word-based and
consistent. The rename may be small enough that its cost is dominated by the
registry and Table S2 rather than by the renaming.
